After a thousand kilometres on the RN7, travellers often ask me: "So where do we stop now?" My answer is one word: Anakao. A Vezo fishing village south of Tulear, a white sand beach as far as the eye can see and dozens of outrigger canoes lined up on the sand. That's where I drop my clients for their last days.

## The Vezo, people of the sea

The Vezo live from fishing all along the south-west coast of Madagascar. Their name comes from a word meaning "to paddle": here, everything is done by canoe. In the morning, square sails head out to the reef; in the afternoon, the fishermen come back and pull their canoes up the beach, right in front of the hotels.

In Anakao, life follows that rhythm. No tarmac, no traffic: just sand, sea and boats. Take time to watch the fishermen mend their nets or carve an outrigger from a trunk: it's the finest show in the village.

## How to get to Anakao

Anakao is reached **by boat from Tulear**. The crossing takes about an hour, depending on the sea. Timetables and prices change with the season and the operator: I always check them the day before for you.

In practice, here is how I do it with my clients:

- I reach Tulear with you at the end of the [RN7](/en/itineraires/rn7-tana-tulear/);
- I drop you at the port the next morning, with your luggage;
- you enjoy Anakao without a car;
- I pick you up on your return and take you to Tulear airport or on to the next road.

There is also a very long sand track overland, for 4x4s only: the boat is almost always the better choice.

## What to do in Anakao

- **Nosy Ve**, the islet facing the village, reached by canoe or boat. It's a nesting site for red-tailed tropicbirds, magnificent seabirds with long red tails, and a good snorkelling spot. Follow the guides' instructions: the birds nest on the ground.
- **A canoe trip with the fishermen**: ask your hotel, they always know a trustworthy boatman.
- **Snorkelling** on the reef, ideally at high tide.
- **Saint-Augustin**, further north, where the Onilahy river meets the sea: a canoe trip to do if the sea is calm.
- **Humpback whales**, which pass along the coast roughly from July to September (the exact season varies, ask locally).

## How many days to stay?

I recommend **two to three nights**. One is too short: with the crossing there and back, you only pass through. Beyond four nights, Anakao becomes a real rest stay, and that's great too.

Accommodation ranges from simple huts to charming bungalows on the sand. In high season, book ahead.

## Anakao or Ifaty?

It's the question I'm asked most often. [Ifaty](/en/guide/ifaty/) is reached by car, an hour north of Tulear, with more choice of hotels and the Reniala spiny forest. Anakao is wilder and quieter, but you need the boat.

My view: if you want authenticity and calm, choose Anakao. If you want to stay mobile and visit a little, choose Ifaty.

## My practical tips

- Take cash in ariary before you go: there is no ATM in the village.
- Bring a dry bag for the crossing, you often get a little wet.
- Pack sunscreen, a hat and water shoes (coral and sea urchins).
- In the evening the light on the canoes is superb: keep your camera charged.
