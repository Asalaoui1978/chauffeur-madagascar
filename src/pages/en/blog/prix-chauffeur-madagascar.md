It's the first question people ask me, often before we even talk about the itinerary. And that makes sense: in Madagascar, hiring a car **with a driver** is the most common way to travel, and transport is a big part of the total cost of the trip. Here is how prices work, straight talk.

## Why almost everyone travels with a driver

In Madagascar, distances are counted in hours, not kilometres. The RN7 is paved but full of potholes, the tracks in the West require knowing how to drive in sand, and sometimes you have to cross rivers by ferry. Most rental companies only offer their vehicles with a driver anyway. The result: the driver is part of the trip, and that's a good thing, because he is also your guide, translator and mechanic.

## What a driver's price (usually) includes

The "per day" price usually covers:

- **the vehicle** (car, minibus or 4x4 depending on the route);
- **the driver** and his know-how;
- **the driver's expenses**: his accommodation and meals for the whole tour.

On the other hand, check whether **fuel** is included. It's the big difference between two quotes. Over a 10-day tour, fuel can add up to a significant amount, especially by 4x4 on tracks.

Usually **not** included: national park entrance fees, the compulsory local guides in the parks, ferry or boat crossings, and of course your own accommodation and meals.

## What price range?

Rates vary with the season (July–August is the busiest period), the type of vehicle and the route. To give you an idea, travellers who share their experience on forums often mention a range of **around €40 to €60 per day for a 4x4 with driver, driver's expenses included, fuel extra**. A saloon car for the RN7 costs less than a 4x4 for the West coast.

These figures are only a guide: the only price that matters is the detailed quote for **your** trip. A very low rate often hides a tired vehicle or "extras" discovered on the road; a very high rate doesn't necessarily mean better service.

## Car or 4x4: it depends on the route

- **RN7 (Tana → Tulear)**: the road is paved, a comfortable car is enough. A 4x4 adds a little comfort on the damaged stretches. [See the RN7 itinerary](/en/itineraires/rn7-tana-tulear/).
- **West coast and Tsingy de Bemaraha**: 4x4 essential, with sand tracks and river crossings. [See the West coast itinerary](/en/itineraires/cote-ouest-tsingy/).
- **Northern road to Nosy Be**: 4x4 recommended, as the RN6 has very rough sections. [See the northern road](/en/itineraires/tana-nosy-be/).

## Questions to ask before booking

1. Is fuel included? If not, how is it calculated (full tank at the start, full tank at the end)?
2. Are the driver's accommodation and meals included?
3. Which vehicle exactly, how many seats, air conditioning?
4. What happens in case of a breakdown?
5. Is the empty return trip charged (for example if you end the trip by flying out of Tulear or Morondava)?
6. Which languages does the driver speak?

A good driver answers all these questions without hesitation, in writing.
