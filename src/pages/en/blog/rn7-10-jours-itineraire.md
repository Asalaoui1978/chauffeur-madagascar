The RN7 is the road everyone wants to drive on a first trip to Madagascar. And for good reason: in just under a thousand kilometres it crosses almost every landscape on the island. I've been driving it for 20 years, and here is how I recommend splitting it into **10 days**, the right pace to see things without rushing.

## Why 10 days (and not 3)

You can get from Tana to Tulear in two or three days of driving. But all you would see is tarmac and potholes. The average speed is around 50 km/h because of villages, zebus and the state of the road, and we never drive at night. Ten days let you walk in the parks, spend two nights at Isalo and finish on the beach.

## Day-by-day itinerary

**Day 1 – Tana → Antsirabe (≈ 170 km).** Early start to avoid the capital's traffic jams. Stop in Ambatolampy at the aluminium foundries. Arrival in Antsirabe, the rickshaw town.

**Day 2 – Antsirabe → Ambositra (≈ 90 km).** In the morning, a rickshaw ride and craft workshops, or a detour to Lake Tritriva in its crater. In the afternoon, Ambositra and its marquetry workshops.

**Day 3 – Ambositra → Ranomafana (≈ 140 km).** We leave the RN7 and the rainforest appears all at once. A dip in the village's hot springs, then a night walk to spot chameleons and mouse lemurs.

**Day 4 – Ranomafana National Park.** A hike with a local guide in search of the golden bamboo lemur.

**Day 5 – Ranomafana → Fianarantsoa → Ambalavao (≈ 120 km).** The old upper town of Fianarantsoa, then Ambalavao and its Antemoro paper.

**Day 6 – Ambalavao → Anja → Isalo (≈ 280 km).** Ring-tailed lemurs at the Anja reserve in the morning, then the crossing of the vast, empty Horombe plateau. Sunset at the Isalo Window.

**Day 7 – Isalo National Park.** The Natural Pool, the Canyon of the Lemurs, a picnic in the canyons.

**Day 8 – Isalo → Tulear (≈ 240 km).** Ilakaka, the sapphire town, a nature break at Zombitse, then arrival facing the Mozambique Channel.

**Days 9 and 10 – Ifaty or Anakao.** Beach, snorkelling and spiny forest at Ifaty, or the Vezo village of Anakao by boat.

All the details, the map and the stops are on the page [RN7: from Tana to Tulear](/en/itineraires/rn7-tana-tulear/).

## The 5 highlights of the RN7

1. **[Ranomafana](/en/guide/ranomafana/)** for the rainforest and lemurs.
2. **[The Anja reserve](/en/guide/ambalavao/)**, run by the villagers, for ring-tailed lemurs.
3. **[Isalo](/en/guide/isalo/)**, its canyons and natural pools.
4. **[Antsirabe](/en/guide/antsirabe/)** for the atmosphere and the crafts.
5. **[Ifaty](/en/guide/ifaty/)** to finish with your feet in the water.

## When to go?

The RN7 can be driven all year round since it is paved, but the ideal period is **April to November**. Be ready for temperature swings: it can be 10 °C in the evening in Antsirabe and 35 °C in Tulear on the same trip.

## And the way back?

Two options: drive back to Tana (two to three days), or **fly back from Tulear**. Another option for the more adventurous: carry on north along the coastal track to Belo-sur-Mer, Morondava and the Tsingy. That's [the West coast itinerary](/en/itineraires/cote-ouest-tsingy/), to be done in the dry season and by 4x4.

## My driver's tips

- Leave early in the morning: the light is beautiful and the road quieter.
- Keep a fleece in the car for the Highlands.
- Carry cash in ariary: ATMs are rare between towns.
- Buy a small cooking pot in Ambatolampy, it's the best souvenir of the RN7.
